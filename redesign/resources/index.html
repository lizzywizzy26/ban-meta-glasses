Ban Smart Glasses — Resources

Smart glasses raise questions about privacy, consent, harassment, safeguarding, data protection and the design of AI-enabled technology. This resource library brings together regulatory documents, government sources, legal analysis, investigations and real-world examples behind the campaign.

UK law, Government policy and regulation

UK Government: no specific smart-glasses legislation currently planned

On 23 June 2026, the UK Government said it had "no current plans to introduce specific legislation" governing the use of camera-enabled smart glasses for filming in public spaces. It pointed instead to existing offences covering conduct including harassment, stalking, voyeurism and non-consensual intimate images, and said it would continue to monitor the issue.

questions-statements.parliament.uk ↗

UK Government: smart glasses, privacy, harassment and abuse

In a further Parliamentary response on 30 June 2026, the Government addressed the potential use of smart glasses for harassment and abuse. It again pointed to existing criminal offences, as well as data protection and online safety legislation.

Ireland and European data protection

Irish Data Protection Commission: concerns about recording notice

In September 2021, Ireland's Data Protection Commission raised concerns about whether the recording indicator on the then Ray-Ban Stories smart glasses gave bystanders adequate notice that they were being recorded. The regulator highlighted an important difference between conventional cameras or phones and glasses: a phone or camera is generally visible as a recording device, while a small indicator light on somebody's glasses may be considerably less obvious.

dataprotection.ie ↗

Meta subsequently changed the product design

In its 2023 Annual Report, the Irish DPC reported that following regulatory engagement Meta had more than doubled the size of the external privacy LED and introduced a blinking recording pattern. The changes were intended to provide more effective notice and minimise the risk of inconspicuous media capture.

dataprotection.ie ↗
